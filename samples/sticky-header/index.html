Sticky header over a woven wall

Scroll. The 3D tiles must pass under the translucent bar as flat 2D — never woven over it, never seaming into it. The bar is ordinary sticky HTML with no inline-3D wiring at all: where the browser occludes by draw order its 2D pixels win over the weave by themselves; otherwise auto-chrome finds it and excludes it from every tile's weave. Same page, both paths — the status line says which one is live. Hit translucency to drop the bar to 45% alpha and watch the wall through it.

The opaque case (browser#167). Hit opaque, then park under the bar: that scrolls a tile until its last strip sits entirely behind the bar. A translucent bar is painted over; an opaque one occludes, so the compositor drops the covered canvas layer altogether and the tile emits no quad at all. Nothing in the bar's own styling changes that — the bug this reproduces was the header's own glyphs coming back interlaced, and no CSS on the header could fix it. Correct behaviour: the bar stays crisp 2D, the parked tile simply stops weaving while it is invisible, and it resumes the moment any of it clears the bar.

One inline-3d session. On a browser with draw-order occlusion enabled the bar needs nothing at all — its 2D pixels win over the weave where they paint. Otherwise auto-chrome registers the sticky bar (and its text) as page-global overlays, so each tile's weave input excludes it and the bar composites as crisp 2D over the woven 3D. Uses the LOCAL js/inline3d.js — this sample exercises the unreleased lifecycle, overlay-guard and occlusion-capability work, so it must not pin a published version. Get the DisplayXR Browser.
